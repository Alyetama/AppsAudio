AppsAudio

Set each app's speaker volume on its own, without changing what gets recorded.

Build from source Requires macOS 14.4 or later · no prebuilt download yet

The point

Mute an app in AppsAudio and it goes quiet on your speakers only. The app keeps producing audio, so anything capturing it — a screen recorder, ScreenCaptureKit, another audio tap — still gets the full signal.

Turn something down in your ears without dropping it from the recording. That's the one thing most volume tricks get wrong.

How it works

macOS 14.4 added Core Audio process taps. When you lower or mute an app, AppsAudio puts a muted tap on it. That pulls the app's audio out of the output mix but leaves the source alone, then re-renders it at your chosen volume through a private aggregate device.

Apps sitting at 100% and unmuted are never tapped. They play untouched, no added latency. The engine only steps in once you actually move a slider.

Settings are saved under the owning app's bundle ID, so an Electron app that plays through helper processes gets one row and one setting rather than several.

Build it

Not published anywhere yet, so there is no clone URL and no release to download. From a copy of the source:

cd AppsAudio
./build_app.sh
cp -R build/AppsAudio.app /Applications/

The build is ad-hoc signed with a stable identity, so macOS remembers the audio-capture permission between launches.

First launch

AppsAudio lives in the menu bar as a small mixer-sliders icon. No Dock icon, no window.

The first time you mute or lower an app, macOS asks "AppsAudio wants to record this computer's audio." Click Allow. This is the system-audio-capture permission, separate from the microphone one — nothing changes volume until you grant it.
